# Floral Industry Size & Growth - Market Research

India’s floriculture/flower market is currently estimated at roughly ₹290–325 billion (INR 29,000–32,500 crore), with medium‑term growth projections in the 10–13% CAGR range, driven mainly by loose flowers for religious/festive use and growing institutional/retail demand. [imarcgroup](https://www.imarcgroup.com/floriculture-market-india)

## Market size (value terms, 2023–2025)

- An IMARC industry report estimates the Indian floriculture market size at INR 292.0 billion in 2024, with value projected to reach INR 744.0 billion by 2033. [imarcgroup](https://www.imarcgroup.com/floriculture-market-india)
- An earlier IMARC analysis put the market at INR 231.7 billion in 2022 and forecasts INR 460.6 billion by 2028, implying a 13.1% CAGR during 2023–2028. [openpr](https://www.openpr.com/news/3099943/indian-floriculture-market-size-worth-inr-460-6-billion-by-2028)
- A Research and Markets summary (based on the same underlying data) reports a market value of INR 323.8 billion in 2025 and INR 793.1 billion by 2034, corresponding to a 10.5% CAGR from 2026–2034. [researchandmarkets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/india-floriculture-market)
- For a longer historical view, IBEF (using MOSPI data) notes that floriculture output was worth Rs 273.5 billion in 2019–20 and grew at a 5.84% CAGR between 2011–12 and 2019–20. [ibef](https://www.ibef.org/research/case-study/india-emerging-as-global-hub-of-horticulture-and-floriculture)
- On the retail side, ICRA (quoted in Hortijournal) estimates the broader retail flower industry—covering cut and loose flowers and organized/unorganized retail—at about ₹15,000 crore in 2023, with expected growth of more than 12% per year and online/app‑based delivery services projected to account for nearly 25% of the urban flower market by 2025. [hortijournal](https://www.hortijournal.com/article/view/379/7-8-23)

Taken together, these industry and government sources suggest a domestic floriculture/flower market in the low‑₹300 billion range by 2024–25, with high‑single‑ to low‑double‑digit growth depending on segment and timeframe. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/indian-floriculture-market-valuation-reach-inr-7931-billion-gupta-rgoic)

## Export market (APEDA)

- APEDA’s floriculture pages, based on National Horticulture Board (NHB) data, report that India exported 22,184.17 metric tonnes of floriculture products in recent years, and that total floriculture exports reached about USD 92.55 million in 2025–26. [apeda.gov](https://apeda.gov.in/FloricultureAndSeeds)
- An IBEF case study on floriculture notes that exports rose from about Rs 1,600 crore in FY21 to roughly Rs 2,000 crore in FY25, reflecting a steady increase in export value alongside domestic market expansion. [ibef](https://www.ibef.org/research/case-study/india-s-floriculture-sector-blooming-with-export-potential-and-rural-prosperity)

## Growth rate and CAGR

- IBEF/MOSPI data indicate historical floriculture output grew at around 5.84% CAGR between 2011–12 and 2019–20. [ibef](https://www.ibef.org/research/case-study/india-emerging-as-global-hub-of-horticulture-and-floriculture)
- IMARC’s 2023–2028 forecast for Indian floriculture implies a 13.1% CAGR, from INR 231.7 billion in 2022 to INR 460.6 billion in 2028. [openpr](https://www.openpr.com/news/3099943/indian-floriculture-market-size-worth-inr-460-6-billion-by-2028)
- IMARC’s newer 2025–2033 forecast (and the Research and Markets summary) project a 10.9–10.5% CAGR over 2025–2033/2026–2034, with market value rising from INR 292.0–323.8 billion to roughly INR 744–793 billion. [researchandmarkets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/india-floriculture-market)

So for a 2023–2024 entrepreneurial lens, most recent industry reports suggest that Indian floriculture is transitioning from earlier mid‑single‑digit growth to a more robust 10–13% projected CAGR over the next decade, especially as organized retail, online delivery, and exports scale up. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/indian-floriculture-market-valuation-reach-inr-7931-billion-gupta-rgoic)
## Key production segments: cut vs loose flowers

Official APEDA/NHB and Statista data give a good picture of production volumes by segment. [statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1039778/floriculture-production-by-state-india/)

### Production and segment share

- APEDA (drawing on NHB and Ministry of Agriculture data) reports that in 2024–25 the area under floriculture in India was about 397.89 thousand hectares, producing 3,226.20 thousand tonnes of loose flowers and 1,039.29 thousand tonnes of cut flowers. [apeda.gov](https://apeda.gov.in/FloricultureAndSeeds)
- An IBEF case study, citing the National Horticulture Database for FY24, notes around 2,85,000 hectares under floriculture, with roughly 22,84,000 MT of loose flowers and 9,47,000 MT of cut flowers. [ibef](https://www.ibef.org/research/case-study/india-s-floriculture-sector-blooming-with-export-potential-and-rural-prosperity)
- Statista data show total floricultural production in India exceeding 4 million metric tonnes in FY2023, with Tamil Nadu as the leading producer (over 1 million MT) and floriculture exports valued at about USD 103 million in FY2022. [statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1039778/floriculture-production-by-state-india/)
- A recent industry outlook based on IMARC data states that loose flowers accounted for about 64% of the total Indian floriculture market in 2025, driven by heavy usage in religious ceremonies, weddings, and festivals. [linkedin](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/indian-floriculture-market-valuation-reach-inr-7931-billion-gupta-rgoic)

## Major seasonal demand periods

### Valentine’s Day and Valentine’s week
- News reports from Himachal Pradesh and national business coverage note that flower demand and prices “almost doubled” during the marriage season and Valentine’s Day, with red rose prices jumping from around ₹30 to ₹50 per stem in the run‑up to Valentine’s. [money.rediff](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/flower-demand-soars-after-marriage-season-valentine-s-day/22220020250213)
- Retail and online players actively market “Valentine’s week” (February 7–14) as a key sales window, with platforms like Farmerr and FNP emphasizing rapid delivery of bouquets and combos across metro areas during this period. [farmerr](https://farmerr.in/collections/valentines-day-flowers)

From a demand perspective, Valentine’s week is one of the sharpest short‑term spikes for cut flowers—especially premium roses—and drives substantial volume for online florists and marketplaces. [upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/demand-for-flowers-goes-up-following-marriage-season-and-valentine-s-day/article-145836/)

### Wedding/marriage season

- Multiple reports highlight that India’s wedding season (especially winter and peak wedding months) triggers a surge in demand for roses, marigolds, and other décor flowers, often resulting in notable price hikes at wholesale markets. [floraldaily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9790227/india-flower-prices-rise-as-wedding-season-increases-demand/)
- Floraldaily notes that ongoing marriage seasons, coupled with temple and individual orders, have led to “doubling” of prices for roses and flower baskets in some markets, underlining how weddings and religious events together drive peak floriculture consumption. [floraldaily](https://www.floraldaily.com/article/9790227/india-flower-prices-rise-as-wedding-season-increases-demand/)

For business planning, wedding season is a sustained demand window—unlike Valentine’s single‑week spike—supporting bulk B2B sales to decorators, venues, and event planners, and heavy use of both cut and loose flowers. [money.rediff](https://money.rediff.com/news/market/flower-demand-soars-after-marriage-season-valentine-s-day/22220020250213)

### Diwali and other major festivals (e.g., Dussehra)

- Cultural and journalistic sources describe marigolds as being “in constant demand” in India, with demand especially high at Diwali; garlands of orange and yellow marigolds adorn homes and temples during the festival of lights. [lovenspire](https://lovenspire.com/blogs/lovenspire-blog-corner/the-role-of-flowers-for-floral-diwali-decoration-decor-with-rangoli-and-marigold-garland-to-celebrate-festival-of-lights)
- Coverage of flower markets in Maharashtra and other states reports that wholesale markets in Pune can receive around 400 truckloads of marigold flowers per day during Diwali, with scarcity in some years pushing prices to ₹400–500 per kg at peak Lakshmi Puja periods. [timesofindia.indiatimes](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/pune/marigold-crop-crisis-heavy-rain-and-disease-set-to-spike-prices-this-diwali/articleshow/114633544.cms)
- Other reports note that large quantities of marigold for Lakshmi Puja cause significant shifts in prices, with marigold, lotus, and chrysanthemum in particularly high demand for Diwali rituals and decoration. [wionews](https://www.wionews.com/videos/india-s-festive-season-what-people-are-buying-this-diwali-1760710116785)

Diwali (along with Dussehra and Navratri) thus creates major seasonal spikes for loose flowers, especially marigolds used in torans, rangoli borders, temple garlands, and household décor, making festival periods critical planning points for growers, wholesalers, and delivery platforms. [dreamaangan](https://dreamaangan.com/5-beautiful-flowering-plants-to-brighten-up-your-home-this-diwali/)
